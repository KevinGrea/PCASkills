# Using Surveys to Improve Your Events

## Why?

As organizers, we all want to make events a great experience for all, run smoothly, and be easier to run. So what should we change for the next time we run an event? We know volunteer time and energy is limited so we want to target the changes and tweaks that have the most impact.

A well crafted post event survey is a great way to get a real understanding of what the event was like for each person there, how smoothly it ran, and what people really valued about it.

It is also lets us get past our own confirmation bias. That bias is human nature and hard to overcome. Do we already *know* what people valued and what should change? We often will initially think of asking simple survey questions that are a paraphrasing of "It was great, wasn't it?". But is doing that useful? 

## What Makes a Useful Survey?

So what makes a survey useful? Do you want to take action or make changes, but you will only know what once you have seen the survey results? If the answer is yes, then that is a good indication the survey is useful. If you already have a change in mind, and are just looking to the survey to confirm that it is needed, what would the survey results need to look like to decide to not make the change? If you can answer that, it is also a good sign that that the survey will be useful.

Another challenge is to understand the conviction behind the feedback received. An open ended "did you encounter any problems?" will always elicit some answer, even if the attendee has to think hard to find something that could have been better, while others might have something they feel very strongly about and reply with firm presses on the keyboard. Can you tell which answers are which? Does every answer warrant changes or even attention?

Below is a well tested survey that has proven to be very useful in the PCA Pacific NorthWest Region (PNWR) Driver Skills series of events.

The trick for determining conviction was borrowed (or should I say stolen?) from a venture capitalist that would use this survey trick to determine if potential consumers have a problem that they would actually pay money to solve, and hence present a market opportunity, versus one where they would say it was indeed problem to be solved, but not one they would actually pay to solve.

## Suggested Post Event Survey

>**PCA PNWR Driver Skills Jan 24th 20XX**
>
> 1. What role did you play in the event? <br>
>*(Required to answer. Single choice. Adjust options to suit your event.)*<br>
> - Participant / Student<br>
> - Volunteer<br>
> - Instructor (or other staff role)
>
> 2. What did you most like? What experiences did you find most valuable? What should we keep doing?<br>
>*(Multi Line Text Answer.)*
>
> 3. What should we change or improve? Tell us what was least valuable or enjoyable about the day.<br>
>*(Multi Line Text Answer.)* 
>
> 4. How strongly do you feel that suggestions or problems you raised in the question above should be addressed? 1 = very minor, 10 = would stop me attending.<br>
>*(**Required** to answer. Number between 1 and 10. Must be required or you will get answers to above question without any conviction measure.)*
>
> 5. How likely are you to recommend attending a future Driver Skills event to a friend or colleague?<br>
>*(Required to answer. [Net Promoter Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_promoter_score). Included only because Microsoft Forms offers this as an industry standardized question so scores can be compared to other events and values eight and below provides another indicator of potentially things to change. Could be dropped as a question.)*
>
> 6. How did you first hear about this event? Check all that apply. <br>
>*(Multiple choice. Have options that make sense for your event advertising channels. Could be dropped as a question if you are not tweaking advertising.)*<br>
> - XXXX.org website<br>
> - Facebook<br>
> - Porsche club members<br>
> - Friends<br>
> - Spiel club magazine<br>
> - PCA PNWR email newsletter<br>
> - MotorsportReg.com<br>
> - Email about Driver Skills<br>
> - Other: *Single text line answer for Other.*
>
> 7. Any other comments or feedback on instructors, food or anything else?<br>
>*(Multi Line Text Answer. Often elicits useful food and other comments. I guess people like a "anything else?")*
>
> 8. What is your name (or skip to remain anonymous)?<br>
> *(Single line Text Answer. Used if follow up needed.)*

When first applied to the PNWR Driver Skills event series, we found these questions uncovered a number of things that needed to be improved that we were unaware of, even though we were at all the events. Often the same thing would be reported by multiple people. As an organizer you can't see everything and experience everything others are experiencing. These open questions let us learn much more then we expected.

 After some time and a number of changes (subtle in some cases) we found those repeated issues and high 1-10 scores dropped away. We still run the survey each event so we can continue to discover, even though most now are almost entirely positive.

Sharing the positive results with the instructors is also highly motivating.

## Getting Responses

Send out the link to the survey as soon as you can after the event. I have found that responses can trickle in for a week, or even more, but the majority will arrive in the first couple of days after the event. You may see popular times for responding, such as early Mondays, or Sunday evenings, when people catch up on their communications.

The length of the survey will have an effect on the number of responses. Keep it to the useful questions, as each question added means fewer responses.

A single link to fill in the survey is most useful, as that can be incorporated into whatever means you use to contact your attendees, be it email, txt, or other messaging system. Here is how PNWR sends out our survey after the event:

>**Subject:** How did we do? PCA PNWR Driver Skills Jan 24th
>
>Now that the cones have been put to bed and new skills have been mulled over, the team would love to know how was your day with us. No matter what your role, let us know by answering this short survey:
>
>https://forms.office.com/r/QaLksJi30E
>
>What did you love and think we should keep? What should we change? We pore over every response. Your feedback has resulted in many changes, and many things being kept the same because of what you tell us is working well.
>
>Thanks, <br>Xxxxx Xxxxxxx, Xxxxx Xxxxxxx, Xxxxx Xxxxxxx <br>Driver Skills Team

## Implementing The Survey

There are multiple services that can be used to deliver such a survey and collect responses. Here are a few. PCA PNWR Driver Skills has been using Microsoft Forms with success.

Don't give away your email or phone number list. You should send the email or text message with the link using your regular system for contacting attendees. The survey services may invite you to paste in the email addresses or phone numbers so the survey service can send the survey on your behalf, but what will they do with the attendee list you give them? Better to not give them your list of attendees than risk inadvertently signing them up for spam or tracking.

If you are using a free service, check for limits such as number of responses allowed or other limitations.

### Microsoft Forms

When using [Microsoft Forms](https://forms.office.com) you can duplicate this
[Microsoft Forms Template](https://forms.office.com/Pages/ShareFormPage.aspx?id=C76A5MFeL0iAy06p8zpoi5ApOm40M15BrQ4tIFNyZIlUMk5LTllHMzUyVEVZOVBPNkEzRFA3MkE0VC4u&sharetoken=YNf10EsuohRFTQgbwU48) and then edit it for your own purposes. Be sure to check the End Date in the Settings. Under Collect Responses we use the Anyone Can Respond option with a shortened URL.

Once responses are coming in, they can be sent to a live syncing Excel spreadsheet, which can then be shared, even to those that don't have a Microsoft 365 subscription. This makes it easier to share survey results with your event team.

PNWR formats that Excel spreadsheet so it can be kept as the final record of the survey and even printed to PDF for wider viewing. An anonymized example of such a spreadsheet is [here](https://pnwr-my.sharepoint.com/:x:/g/personal/driverskills_pnwr_org/IQCuesrpRWrCQKvd8fAIfk2_Aa3Yo_tWoGslFQUvEwEjirU?e=qNEWLc "Post event survey results for PCA PNWR Driver Skills Sample") and it can be used to copy formatting to your own. Use the format painter command, hide irrelevant columns, add a heading row, adjust justifications (low on headings, high on data), set column widths, set wrapping on all cells with much text, use conditional formatting on numeric data, set the repeating rows on sheet settings for printing so headings repeat, and adjust the printing margins and settings to show all columns on a single landscape page.

### Google Forms

[Google Forms](https://forms.google.com) can also be used. Check limitations on free accounts.

### Survey Monkey

Survey Monkey's free offering used to support all that was needed for the survey questions above, but that changed years ago, and now even this basic survey would require a paid account. PNWR moved off of Survey Monkey to Microsoft Forms.
